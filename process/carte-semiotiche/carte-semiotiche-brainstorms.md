### Museum as Technique

I wanted to celebrate the assets of Maksim Bugrimov, and Maksim Bugrimov as well, I wanted to think about what assets are and what they mean on their own, I wanted make my own meanings with assets outside games, I wanted to share with player the sense of magic in seeing behind the curtain of the world, I wanted to show them the beauty of a shrub asset the way Chad saw it that day in Alan Wake. The only way I can really get my thoughts straight about all those ideas is to make a game that tries to communicate them. And so I made a musem of videogame assets made by Maksim Bugrimov in a videogame.

Videogames are sealed. They are sealed especially for players who mostly have no way at all of seeing behind or through or into the world behind the world they play in. Even for those of us who make games, manipulate the assets, rev the game engines, it is all too easy to seal ourselves into a vision only of the world to be, the world we are bringing about, and not to see the very pieces and materials and stuff we are working with.

The seal is a problem because videogames are important and we all deserve to think about and encounter their inner nature. The seal is a problem because all technology is important and dominant and authoritarian and we all deserve to think about and encounter its inner nature. We need ways to step through the hard, polished surfaces of software to see inside. We need looking glasses to go through and games are looking glasses that can give us entry not just into their worlds, but into themselves.

A videogame can be a museum of videogames. A museum in a videogame can show and expose the materials of videogames, its own materials, for anyone who visits. Players can spend time estranged from play and playthings to look at the pieces of their worlds up close and unfamiliar. Makers can spend time estranged from their tools and objects to look at the pieces of their worlds up close and unfamiliar. This is museum as technique.

When we can walk inside the walls of the games we play, when we can find them strange again, when the bend of a bush in a forest is a marvel, then we can come to new understandings. We can see more clearly, play more deeply, and make things new.

Some things...

- Games are monolithic, sealed, total, but secretly full of individual craft and craftspeople
- Assets are generative and interesting to think about, they *suggest games*, they inevitably function as artworks when isolated because they are artworks
- A museum is a strange place, estranging the things inside it, telling us to see them, tripping us up over them; a museum in a videogame is doubly estranged because it is unusual, anti-agency, passive, boring, limited, ...
- A videogame museum of videogame assets might be triple estrangement
- Museums are or can be places for comparison, estrangement makes comparison possible, detaching things from the world
- Collecting assets by a single creator enables deeper reflections and comparisons; implied worlds, costs, techniques, palettes, thoughts, subjects, complexities, weights, genres, ...
- Curation and installation of assets in a museum is like a game, imagining how to present them less as natural history and more as art, art-supporting, art materials, admissible into a new scenario outside either the presumed (Stalin Statue on a desk in a Soviet era videogame setting) or museum (Stalin statue on a plinth) setting.

Designers need estranging to reimagine possibilities for assets, to resist or at least see the implications of assets as only one choice. Assets need estranging to expand their lives and values.
